The Dissecting Dual

Every figurate number here is counted twice. Assembled, it is a lattice of dots stacked in layers, and its closed form counts them. Dissected, the same solid splits into tetrahedral atoms, and the Kim identity counts the dots atom by atom.

Tetrahedral numbers: Tn=n(n+1)(n+2)6 and Tn=Tn

The rail moves between the two: Assemble at one end, Dissect at the other.

Kim identity

The atoms come from Theorem 4.1 of Kim and Lee (2012). Cut a polytope into tetrahedra that all meet at one corner, a pointed triangulation, and its figurate numbers split into tetrahedral numbers:

P(n)=Tn+a1Tn−1+a2Tn−2

Every dot is counted by exactly one atom. Where two atoms share a face, its dots go to one of them, so each atom leaves some faces open, and an atom with k open faces counts Tn−k dots: one term of the identity.

Tetrahedral numbers: Tn=Tn, 1 atom.

The tetrahedron is its own single atom, with no face open, so Tn=Tn: the atom that comes out of the lattice is the whole figure.

Each atom adds one term, so there are 1 + a1 + a2 atoms, the same number as the third difference Δ³P(n):

For the tetrahedron, square pyramid, octahedron and cube, Ehrhart theory, which counts the lattice points of a polytope and of its dilates, calls the coefficients 1, a1, a2 the polytope’s h*-vector, and the number of atoms its normalized volume.

Kim Mode lays the atoms over the lattice; move toward Dissect and they come out of it. Kim Mode Only shows the atoms alone, with a dashed gold outline where the solid stood. Point at a term of the identity at the top of the page, or at its number, to see its atoms and light the dots they count.

H. K. Kim and J. Y. Lee, “Polytope numbers and their properties,” arXiv:1206.0511 (2012). doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1206.0511

Kim Mode is this viewer’s name for the dissected views, after H. K. Kim’s work on polytope numbers. It is not a term from the paper.
